# Proposed contribution: joint particle identity, void ownership, and aggregate assembly

Research design dated October 4, 2026. This is a candidate algorithm, not an implemented method or a verified novelty/performance claim. Existing production files are unchanged.

**Recommendation.** Develop one coupled resolver that infers which masks describe the same primary particle, which distinct particles belong to the same visible connected group, and whether an enclosed region belongs to one particle or lies between several particles. The potentially original contribution is the bidirectional ownership criterion: void evidence changes primary identities; primary identities change void interpretation. The graph solver, morphology, SAM, and contour fitting remain cited components.

**The actual problem.** The current containment forest selects one parent per proposal. `type_children` can assign an aggregate only when an enclosing proposal already exists. The amendments explicitly record the missing parent on `pylypchuk2021_greenchem_04058d__p05_x46__panel00`, and the two-sphere mask without inner proposals on panel17. The same amendments record a former four-sphere outline whose interstitial gap was interpreted as its cavity. These motivate a joint problem rather than another isolated threshold.

Cached `runs/mvp_v2` output for tracking panel00 reports 17 particles and zero aggregates. Those counts are pipeline outputs, not ground truth. No particle/aggregate/cavity ground-truth masks were found under `data/gt` in this inspection; those labels must be supplied for a real evaluation.

**What prior work already covers.** [Monteiro et al. (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-86327-x) already assign SAM lobe masks to the same multi-lobed particle by minimizing centroid distance. Parent-free grouping alone is therefore insufficient novelty. [Pape et al. (2019)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2019.00006/full) already incorporate domain constraints into microscopy graph partitioning. [Park et al. (2013)](https://aml.engr.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/164/2017/11/J43.pdf) already separate overlapping nanoparticles and infer missing contours. [Mishra and Aloimonos (2011)](https://www.umiacs.umd.edu/research/SRVC/NSF-project/papers/SimpleObjects_rss2011.pdf) discuss object and hole boundary ownership. None of these ingredients should be presented as newly invented here. A targeted search did not identify this exact coupled formulation, which is weaker evidence than a comprehensive priority review.

1. **Represent three decisions separately.** A SAM proposal is an observation, not automatically a particle. Give each supported proposal patch a provisional `primary_id`, and each distinct primary an optional `group_id`. Give each void an interpretation `cavity(primary_id)`, `interstitial_gap(primary_ids)`, or `uncertain`. Several patches can share one primary; several primaries can share one group. An enclosing SAM mask is optional. Raw masks and image pixels determine material/void evidence; filled masks remain geometric hypotheses, not proof that an enclosed region is material.

   Keep credible unresolved masks in the hypothesis pool. Retain near-duplicate variants as alternative boundaries, with one vote per proposal family so correlated duplicates do not inflate evidence. Exclusion pixels are never supporting evidence or connecting pixels. Identity assignments do not force a disjoint pixel label image: accepted overlapping or stacked particle masks remain whole.

2. **Recover uncovered body material before resolving contacts.** The initial version only tested short inter-mask corridors. That partially handles uncovered connectors but does not reconstruct broad uncovered body regions or measure how essential those regions are. The following explicit residual layer is needed for that case.

   In a fixed local neighborhood, let `U` be the union of credible raw particle/body proposals, excluding background, gap and cavity proposals. Independently estimate an image-supported material region `F` from signed local contrast and exterior-boundary evidence. Use candidate material bands as growth anchors, but allow image-supported growth outside every SAM mask. This foreground/body estimator must use the image, not assume that `U` is the complete object. Respect exclusion and visible-background barriers; maintain unknown pixels separately. Evaluate several contrast settings and alternate mask families rather than treating one estimate as truth.

   The residual is `R = F minus U`. Its connected components become explicit nodes alongside proposal patches. Connect each residual node to the patches it touches through supported material. A residual component may connect three or more patches directly, avoiding a restriction to pairwise narrow corridors. Assemble a body hypothesis from supported patches and the complete residual regions; do not retain only shortest connector paths.

   Record two distinct quantities: uncovered body fraction `|R|/|F|`, and residual connectivity dependence. For the latter, compare which supported patch anchors share a connected component in `F` with which share one in `F intersect U`, after deleting residual material. Count lost anchor connections rather than every tiny pixel component. An essential thin bridge can have small area and large connectivity impact; broad omitted material can have large area without changing connectivity. Both are informative.

   Test sensitivity to small boundary dilations of `U`, alternate SAM boundaries, and foreground thresholds so ordinary segmentation seams do not automatically become essential residual connectors. Do not count holes enclosed by filled masks as observed material. Estimate background outside the expanded candidate body; if newly grown material occupies the original background sectors, invalidate that estimate and mark the hypothesis uncertain or recompute from independently supported exterior sectors.

   This distinguishes residual-supported body assembly from a touching-particle group whose existing masks account for nearly all observed material. It measures SAM undercoverage and its structural importance. It cannot, by itself, prove fusion: a missed particle in an ordinary touching group can produce the same residual dependence. Cavity ownership and independently supported primary boundaries still determine the interpretation. Body reconstruction does not require confirmed primary identities; multiplicity may remain unknown.

   **Compute local contact witnesses as additional evidence.** For each nearby pair, use a short corridor around their closest facing boundaries. Its length and width limits scale with the smaller primary diameter; it cannot detour through the rest of the panel. For each evaluated explanation, block exclusion pixels, its assigned voids, and its accepted third bodies when evaluating a direct connection. Mutually exclusive alternative masks are not blockers. A path through a third accepted primary is represented by two graph edges. Broad residual regions are represented by the residual nodes above, not forced into these pairwise corridors.

   Freeze a proposal-based neighborhood and halo before optimization, excluding obvious background proposals. Estimate background from exterior sectors outside this neighborhood's candidate-mask union, with adequate unmasked support. The estimated background does not change with the winning group assignment. Define signed material evidence

   \[
   z(x)=\frac{s\,[I(x)-b_{\mathrm{local}}]}{\max(\sigma_{\mathrm{local}},\epsilon)},
   \]

   where `s` is the modality polarity and `sigma_local` is a robust background MAD scale. This is a contrast statistic, not a calibrated material probability. If exterior sectors are unavailable or disagree strongly, mark the witness uncertain. A global estimate may be a separately flagged fallback; it must not silently replace missing local evidence.

   For a small fixed grid of contrast levels `t` and bridge widths `w`, test whether the corridor has a connecting path in the eroded superlevel set `{z >= t}`. Seed endpoints come from material-side bands of the two hypotheses, not their potentially background-like interiors. Record the fraction of tests that connect, and repeat across alternate proposal boundaries. Compare to nearby background corridors of matched geometry. Threshold/width sweeps and connectivity are standard tools; their use alone is not the contribution.

   This witness supports a visible connected group. It does not distinguish fusion, touching, projected overlap, or blur. A sharp internal seam may support two distinct primaries while both retain the same group ID.

3. **Test who explains each void.** Use SAM cavity/gap proposals and enclosed background-like image regions as candidates; do not require a SAM void proposal. For a void `V`, sample its perimeter `Gamma(V)` at fixed arc-length intervals. Freeze observable samples from image contrast, border and exclusion visibility before considering candidate owners. At each sample, trace a bounded corridor along the smoothed void's outward normal. A candidate `p` explains the sample only when its raw-mask evidence supports the initial material-side band, the corridor reaches `p`'s hypothesized exterior, and an independently detected particle-to-background transition occurs near that endpoint. The corridor cannot borrow a rim belonging to another accepted primary. Mark the sample unpaired if no such transition is observed; never extend indefinitely to find one.

   A useful proposed statistic is

   \[
   Q(p,V)=\frac{\text{length of void boundary supported by }p\text{ and a paired outer contour}}{\text{length of the observable void boundary}}.
   \]

   Use the same observable denominator for every `p`. Always report observable coverage alongside `Q`; a high score on a tiny visible arc cannot certify a cavity. Compute image ridges and supported division candidates before unioning masks. The score must also be penalized when `p` has poor independent outer-contour support or crosses one of these supported internal divisions. A union of neighboring spheres can supply paired contours too, so pairing alone cannot prevent a spurious single owner. If the image cannot discriminate these explanations, the correct outcome is uncertain ownership.

   Compare two complete explanations: one primary with a paired inner/outer shell, versus several independently supported primaries whose exterior arcs surround an interstitial space. Include the current centrality and absolute background-like tests as domain assumptions for an observable opening. A centered dark region alone is insufficient. These are image-based hollow-opening labels within the existing scope, not proof of a three-dimensional hollow particle.

4. **Resolve the explanations jointly.** For a small ambiguous neighborhood, enumerate the existing primary hypotheses and a bounded number of alternative fragment unions or split hypotheses. Choose primary identities `P`, group relations `G`, and void roles `O` together using

   \[
   E(P,G,O)=E_{\mathrm{outline}}(P)
   +\lambda_cE_{\mathrm{contact}}(G\mid P)
   +\lambda_vE_{\mathrm{ownership}}(O\mid P)
   +\lambda_hE_{\mathrm{unsupported}}(P,G).
   \]

   All terms use normalized lengths/areas. `E_outline` scores outer-contour support and supported primary separation; `E_contact` scores contact-witness agreement; `E_ownership` scores the complete single-owner versus multi-owner explanations; `E_unsupported` penalizes invented outlines and connecting material. Weights, coverage requirements, candidate limits, and an ambiguity margin are selected on development data and then frozen. The equation defines a design family; the implemented term definitions must be published before testing.

   One concrete initial scoring choice is: (a) mean unsupported exterior-boundary fraction plus the fraction of independently supported divisions crossed by each primary; (b) `1-W(p,q)` for an accepted contact and `W(p,q)` for a rejected contact, where `W` is the contact-witness sweep fraction; (c) `1-Q(p,V)` for a cavity, versus uncovered perimeter plus a penalty when fewer than two independently supported identities explain an interstitial gap; (d) unsupported added material area divided by the local observed body area. The uncertain void state has a fixed development-selected cost. Retain the separation penalty in all ownership models so a union cannot erase the evidence that made its constituents distinct. These are heuristic costs, not statistical log likelihoods or confidence probabilities.

   For the first prototype, bound generation to individual candidate masks, unions of at most three neighboring complementary patches, and at most two supplied split candidates per compound mask. Evaluate these on the same fixed image evidence, preserving mutual exclusivity and overlap rules. This finite candidate set limits what the algorithm can discover; failure to enumerate a correct primary is an explicit limitation.

   Crucially, `E_ownership` is a multi-object factor. A credible single-owner shell favors joining patches that merely fragment one particle. A credible multi-owner gap favors retaining the surrounding particles as distinct identities, although they may share a group. Joint evaluation is essential: ordinary clustering followed by a cavity label does not implement the proposed contribution.

   Exhaustive search over small neighborhoods is a reasonable first implementation. A general graph optimizer can come later and must be cited. If the best two explanations are close, retain `uncertain` rather than manufacture a cavity or particle split. Do not penalize uncertainty so heavily that the solver always invents a confident answer.

5. **Create a virtual aggregate with traceable support.** Define groups as connected components of accepted visible-contact edges between distinct primary identities. Require the configured minimum number of confirmed distinct primaries to label a component an aggregate; a patch union explaining one primary is a particle. A connected body whose multiplicity is unknown receives a separate unresolved-body output, not an invented member count.

   Construct its observed material map from image-supported portions of accepted raw body proposals plus complete accepted residual regions and supported connecting pixels, then refine its external outline against the original image in a narrow band. Keep material, void, and unknown regions separate: neither raw SAM membership nor hole filling alone certifies material. Contour/owner evidence can explain a background-like shell interior; otherwise it stays unknown. The residual layer can recover material outside all SAM proposals when the image supports it; neither it nor contact witnesses can recover a boundary that the image does not resolve. Derive a separate filled exterior envelope for the current aggregate diameter convention; never feed that filling back as material evidence.

   Preserve independently measurable primaries within the group. Label remaining body patches `aggregate_fragment`, assign their aggregate ID, and exclude them from primary size statistics. Do not report a fragment count as a primary-particle count. If complete primary boundaries cannot be identified, report the aggregate outline and the confirmed primary count, with unresolved material remaining. This changes the current output schema and scope explicitly; it needs a corresponding spec amendment if adopted.

**A second, bounded algorithm: cavity-aware split acceptance.** This addresses panel17's two-sphere mask without child proposals. Generate candidate cuts using an existing distance-transform/contour method and cite it. Score the single-primary and two-primary explanations with the same ownership factor. Reject a cut that destroys a well-supported single-owner shell without evidence of a second primary. Favor a cut that creates two supported outer contours and explains an enclosed region as their interstitial gap. Return uncertain when neither explanation wins clearly. The prospective novelty is the void-conditioned acceptance criterion, not watershed, concavity detection, or contour completion. This is an extension to test after the aggregate resolver, since it requires additional candidate generation.

**Why this is plausible, and what has actually been checked.** A read-only contact probe on cached masks found locally material-like connectors in the tracking SEM panel, so missing aggregate parents do not imply missing contact evidence. The TEM control also has strong connector evidence and weak internal interfaces. That comparison disproves the shortcut that a strong bridge and weak seam identify fusion. The probe additionally found contamination of local background annuli inside the dense cluster. These observations motivate separate identity/group variables and exterior-sector normalization. They are feasibility diagnostics, not validation of the proposed algorithm. The temporary probe files are `/private/tmp/lnp_feasibility_probe.py` and `/private/tmp/lnp_feasibility_probe_output.txt`; its illustrative corridor calculation can intersect third proposals and is not the specified assembler.

**Falsifiable evaluation.** Use the known panels only for development. Annotate new panels with visible primary outlines, aggregate envelopes, void roles and ambiguity, and split evaluation by source paper. Compare the current resolver, centroid grouping, ordinary contact grouping, and the full coupled method on the same proposals. Include ablations removing residual-region recovery, residual connectivity dependence, void-to-identity feedback, contact evidence, alternate-mask checks, and local normalization. Test single hollow particles, solid particles with rim shading, interstitial gaps, fused-looking clusters, near neighbors separated by background, exclusions, and truncated examples. Also deliberately remove one particle proposal from a touching group: this should increase undercoverage uncertainty, not serve as proof of fusion.

Report aggregate IoU and boundary error, grouping precision/recall, cavity-versus-gap F1, primary count and diameter error, and accuracy versus abstention rate. Use paper-level uncertainty estimates; the many particles within one paper are not independent evidence of generalization. In a deliberate parent-deletion test, remove enclosing cluster proposals and measure recovery from the remaining data. Synthetic tests establish behavior under controlled assumptions, while real held-out micrographs establish useful performance.

**Decision rule for the research direction.** Continue only if the joint method improves aggregate recovery and false-cavity decisions on held-out papers while preserving credible individual measurements. The central novelty claim requires a measurable benefit from the ownership coupling over the same method without it. If ordinary contact grouping explains the gain, report that finding and reconsider the proposed contribution. The generalization target remains the current in-scope observable SEM/TEM particles; transparent thin-shell TEM interpretation is a separate unresolved problem.

**Prospective methods claim, conditional on literature review and experiments:** “We introduce a joint particle–void ownership criterion that distinguishes single-particle openings from interparticle spaces and uses these assignments to resolve primary-particle identities and construct aggregate masks without requiring an enclosing segmentation proposal.”
